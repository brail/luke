# ADR-035 — Date and Time Model: Calendar Days, Instants and Two Zone Roles

## Status

Accepted

## Context

Planning is a core function of Luke: season calendars, deadlines, working days, digests and exports all depend on what day and time a value means. The pieces of a model already exist — `CalendarDate` and the zone-aware helpers in `packages/core/src/utils/dateUtils.ts`, `CalendarDateSchema` and `CalendarInstantSchema` in `packages/core/src/schemas/dates.ts`, the business time zone for deadlines and working days described in `docs/country-aware-working-days.md` — but no record states it as a rule, and the code reads dates in three zones, and in places in UTC or in the zone of the process.

- The API reads deadlines, lateness, locks and working days in the business zone (`app.defaultTimezone`), and exports, digests, maintenance notices and the audit-log filter in the profile zone of the user (`User.timezone`, resolved on the server by `apps/api/src/lib/userTimeZone.ts`).
- The web renders the calendar views, takes event times, schedules maintenance and formats about twenty-five other values in the zone of the browser, while `useFormatDate` formats in the profile zone. A user whose browser zone differs from the profile sees different days in different places, sometimes in the same dialog.
- Calendar days are sent as instants by some inputs (vendor closures, the template anchor), and are correct only because the web happens to send UTC midnight.
- Bounds disagree: `CalendarDateSchema` stops at 9999-12-30 so that every day has a next one, while `CalendarInstantSchema` accepts 9999-12-31, on which the deadline helpers throw. Dates generated by template application and calendar cloning are not checked before they are written: out of bounds they fail as a `RangeError` or a database error rather than a refusal with a message.
- Day arithmetic is done by hand: calendar cloning shifts by fixed 24-hour blocks, so a timed event changes its wall time across a daylight-saving change.
- Two formatting modules coexist; one of them (`utils/date.ts`) falls back silently to the zone of the process or browser when the zone it is given is invalid.
- `Europe/Rome` is written as a fallback zone in two places other than the business-zone default: the fallback of `useFormatDate` and the database default of `User.timezone`.

Alternatives considered:

- **The browser zone as the reader zone in the web**, the profile zone only on the server. Rejected: two rules, and the web would disagree with exports, digests and the audit-log filter whenever the profile differs from the browser.
- **A date library or a `Temporal` polyfill.** Rejected: core already has DST-correct conversions (`calendarDateIn`, `startOfDayIn`, `instantAt`), no workspace depends on a date library, and `Temporal` is not native in Node 24 nor in every supported browser.
- **Calendar days typed as `YYYY-MM-DD` in API outputs as well as inputs.** Deferred: outputs keep sending stored calendar days as ISO instants at UTC midnight, read with `calendarDateOf`.

## Decision

### Four primitives

| Concept | In memory | On the wire (input) | Storage | Schema |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calendar day | `CalendarDate` | `YYYY-MM-DD` | `@db.Date`, or a timestamp at UTC midnight with `allDay` (an encoding, not an instant) | `CalendarDateSchema` |
| Instant | `Date` | ISO 8601 in UTC (`Z`) | `timestamp(3)` | `CalendarInstantSchema` |
| Wall time | `HH:mm` | `HH:mm` | a string in AppConfig | `WallTimeSchema` |
| Time zone | IANA name, in the letter case `Intl` gives it (`canonicalTimeZone`); aliases kept as written | IANA name | a string | `TimeZoneSchema` |

Every date schema and helper lives in `@luke/core`: `schemas/dates.ts` for the schemas, `utils/dateUtils.ts` for conversion, arithmetic and formatting, and domain rules built on them next to it (`utils/calendarEventLock.ts` for deadlines and locks). A calendar day is never read as an instant: its UTC-midnight encoding is not a moment. A day becomes an instant only by naming the zone and the wall time (`startOfDayIn`, `instantAt`), and an instant becomes a day only by naming the zone (`calendarDateIn`).

### Two zone roles

- **The business zone** (`app.defaultTimezone`) is the company's day. It decides deadlines, lateness, working days, locks, deadline notices, the dashboard's weekly sales, calendar cloning, template application and the company's scheduler slots (the daily backup).
- **The reader zone** is the profile zone of the person, as the server resolves it (`getUserTimeZone`, falling back to the business zone) and `me.get` returns it. It decides everything else a person reads or types: web views and inputs, exports, emails, and digests, which are also sent at an hour of the recipient's zone.
- **The browser zone never reads an instant for a signed-in person.** It proposes a profile update (`TimezoneUpdateDialog`), and it dates instants for a reader without a session — the sign-in page, the maintenance banner shown there — who has no profile zone yet; the value names the zone, and a countdown is written as a relative time.
- The zones of the clocks widget are zones the user names explicitly. They are validated like any zone and are neither role.
- Two zones are the same when the names `Intl` resolves them to in the same runtime are equal, not when their strings are (`Asia/Kolkata` and `Asia/Calcutta`).

### Moving and saving

- Shifting by N days has one contract, in core. An all-day event moves both of its days by calendar arithmetic. A timed event moves its start keeping the wall time in a named zone, to the millisecond — a wall time the target day skips is read with the offset before the gap, as RFC 5545 and `Temporal`'s `compatible` do, so it lands as far past the gap as it was past the hour; a repeated one keeps its occurrence where it exists, else the first — and its end follows at the original duration, so the interval never inverts; across a daylight-saving change the end's wall time moves by the difference, which is preferred to an end that could fall before its start. N = 0 is the identity. Shifting an instant by `N × 86 400 000` ms is not a calendar shift.
- A form rebuilds an instant from a day and a wall time only when the user changed them; saving an unchanged value sends back the stored instant, so an event in the second occurrence of a repeated hour does not move.
- A time typed in a form is resolved by `instantAt`: a skipped wall time becomes the first instant after the gap, a repeated one its first occurrence. Every core helper that resolves a wall time states how it treats both; callers do not choose their own.

### Bounds

- Calendar days: 1900-01-01 to 9999-12-30, so every day has a next one.
- Instants: their UTC date is a valid calendar day, `[1900-01-01T00:00Z, 9999-12-31T00:00Z)`, which also covers all-day values stored at UTC midnight.
- Timed instants a person enters — timed events and any other: `[1900-01-02T00:00Z, 9999-12-30T00:00Z)`, so that in every zone from UTC−12 to UTC+14 their day exists and has a next one. For events the check runs where their dates are written (`assertEventDates`), on the effective `allDay`, rather than in the shared schemas; any other instant input carries it in its own schema.
- Generated dates — template application, calendar cloning — pass the same check. A timed template item starts at the start of its day in the business zone. A result out of bounds is refused with a message, never a `RangeError`.
- Database CHECK constraints hold calendar values to the same bounds.
- Dates no constraint covers — inside audit metadata, or from an external source such as the holiday import or NAV — are validated where they are read or imported. A reader degrades one malformed value instead of failing the whole result, as the calendar digest does with audit metadata.

### External dates

NAV's blank date, `1753-01-01` (the minimum of SQL Server's `datetime`), is a missing value: the sync stores it as `null`.

### The reader zone in the web

- The hook that provides the reader zone exposes whether it is ready. Until it is, labels show a placeholder, and conversions, form initialisation, submissions and client-side exports wait; an error is reported, never replaced by another zone.
- A form captures the zone when it opens, names it next to its time fields and converts with it, so a profile change while the form is open does not reinterpret what was typed.
- Code outside React receives the zone and the locale as arguments.

### Formatting

- Instants are formatted only by core functions, with an explicit zone; an invalid zone is an error, never a silent fallback. Calendar days are formatted without a zone.
- Until the i18n cycle every date is formatted in one locale, the core default (`it-IT`): the web reads it through the reader-zone hook and the server from core, so the cycle changes one place on each side.
- The audit-log export writes instants in UTC and says so; other exports write them in the reader zone and name it. The audit-log rows are still selected by days of the reader zone, so a row near midnight carries a UTC date outside the chosen days.

### New users

A new user takes the business zone when the user is created. `User.timezone` has no database default: a second copy of the business-zone default would not follow it when it changes. Stored zones are left as they are.

### Enforcement

An ESLint rule of the `@luke` plugin refuses, in source files outside core's date modules, the zone-implicit forms it can recognise without type information; the rule and its fixtures hold the list. It starts from the local getters and setters of `Date`, `toDateString`, `toTimeString`, `toLocaleDateString` and `toLocaleTimeString`, `new Date` with several arguments or with a string written or assembled in place (a literal without `Z`, a template, a concatenation), `Date.parse`, `getTimezoneOffset`, `Intl.DateTimeFormat`, `toISOString().slice(0, 10)` and `z.string().datetime()`. `toLocaleString`, which numbers share, and the forms a syntax rule cannot tell apart are left to review. Tests are outside the rule: they build fixtures in the zone of the process on purpose. Each exemption carries its reason; `apps/api/scripts/fix-allday-event-dates.ts`, which repairs dates during the 2.1 → 3.0 upgrade in the zone of the process by design, and `TimezoneUpdateDialog`, which reads the browser zone by design, are exempt.

### Not decided here

- The output format of calendar days (see the deferred alternative).
- Which NAV portfolio columns can hold the blank date, and their nullability; the season sync is the first to follow the rule above.
- Locales other than the core default, which belong to the i18n cycle, and with them `app.locale`, a registry key nothing reads.
- The `@luke/core/utils/date` subpath, which [ADR-022](022-core-package-export-boundary.md) publishes: when the instant formatters leave `utils/date.ts`, the subpath is either repointed or retired, and retiring it supersedes ADR-022.

## Consequences

- One reading of a date per person: with a browser zone that differs from the profile, the calendar, the event dialog, digests, the audit-log filter and every export but the audit log's, which states UTC, show the same days and times, and forms name the zone they use.
- The calendar views are built on calendar days rather than on the local `Date` of the browser, and their tests run with a browser zone that differs from the reader zone.
- Deadline notices stay in the business zone for every recipient: a deadline is the company's, as the criticality badges already show.
- An application older than this decision cannot create users on a schema without the `User.timezone` default: a rollback that keeps the database must restore it too.
- A reviewer's question about a date is which of the four primitives it is and which zone role reads it; code that cannot answer names neither.

### Observed gaps

These are the main deviations that exist when this record is accepted, not an inventory: the follow-up work keeps the full census. They are being removed as follow-ups; this record does not endorse them.

- The calendar views, event inputs, scheduled maintenance and the other direct formatting and local `Date` reads in the web (the dashboard greeting and week, among others) use the browser zone, and `useFormatDate` falls back to a hardcoded `Europe/Rome`.
- The instant formatters live in `utils/date.ts`, published as `@luke/core/utils/date`; it formats instants without a zone (`formatDate`) or falls back silently on an invalid one (`formatDateWithTimezone`).
- Vendor closures and the template anchor accept instants for calendar days; several schemas use `z.string().datetime()` directly; the clocks-widget zones are not validated.
- `CalendarInstantSchema` accepts 9999-12-31, and timed events, template results and clones are not held to the bounds above.
- Calendar cloning shifts by 24-hour blocks; `@luke/calendar` computes the next day with `Date.UTC`; storage key partitions and the copyright year use local getters.
- `useFormatDate` formats in the locale of the profile, the server in `it-IT`.
- The holiday import stores the dates of its external source without validating them, one row at a time.
- Zones are compared as strings: `TimezoneUpdateDialog` prompts every session for an alias pair, and `groupByTimeZone` puts aliases in separate digest and maintenance cohorts.
- The API formats some instants with `Intl.DateTimeFormat` directly (export timestamps, the greeting hour); the forex widget takes "yesterday" as the UTC date 24 hours ago; template application writes timed items at UTC midnight.
- Exports other than the audit log do not name the zone they write in (the collection-layout and pricing-grid PDF headers, the season-calendar PDF and XLSX).
- `User.timezone` defaults to `Europe/Rome` in the database, and the CHECK constraints cover only `calendar_events` and `planning_groups`, up to the year 9999.
- The season sync stores NAV's blank date `1753-01-01` as a date.
- Parts of this decision do not exist yet: the `@luke` ESLint rule, `WallTimeSchema` and `TimeZoneSchema` (zones are validated inline only, by the profile schema and the `app.defaultTimezone` registry entry), the shared shift helper (core has `addCalendarDays` only), and the business zone for new users (the creators set no `timezone`, so the database default applies).
